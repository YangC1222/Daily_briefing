English abstract

Recent development of the LEU+ fuel supply chain enables new pathways to extend discharge burnup in light water SMRs, whose burnup is often reactivity-limited due to high neutron leakage. This study investigates LEU+ fueled core concepts for an i-SMR-based SMR with the objective of reducing annual spent fuel assembly discharge by approximately 30 % relative to the reference i-SMR with U-235 enrichment below 5 %, which corresponds to extending the cycle length from 730 to 1043 EFPD while maintaining the same number of feed fuel assemblies. Two variants are designed and assessed: a soluble-boron-free (SBF) core and a soluble-boron-used (SBU) core. For the SBF design, increased reliance on burnable absorbers and control rods motivates adoption of a 17 × 17 assembly with 28 guide tubes and 1 instrumentation tube, together with multiple IGD absorber options including enriched gadolinium configurations. Although a carefully designed shuffling scheme in the SBF core can effectively mitigate hoop stresses, control rod-driven axial depletion non-uniformity increases the peak nodal burnup to 80 MWd/kgU at rod average burnup below 65 MWd/kgU, accelerating fission gas release and rod internal pressure at high local burnup. In the SBU design, soluble boron provides cycle-long global reactivity control, reducing reliance on control rod insertion, yielding more uniform depletion behavior with moderated peak nodal burnup and fuel performance trends. Overall, the results support the technical viability of LEU+ for long-cycle SMR from the standpoint of neutronics and fuel performance under normal operation; however, the SBF implementation may induce increasing control rod-induced depletion non-uniformity when pursuing additional burnup extension beyond the burnup levels evaluated in this study (65 MWd/kgU).

English abstract

Premixing during a fuel–coolant interaction determines the three-dimensional melt–water–vapor state available to a subsequent steam explosion. This study develops and validates the premixing-phase model of the three-dimensional fuel–coolant interaction code SEINA-3D. The model advances five coupled Eulerian material fields—liquid coolant, vapor, connected melt jet, melt droplets, and melt fragments—in one pressure-based framework. Mixed finite-volume/finite-difference discretization, explicit/implicit advancement, and improved SIMPLEPC pressure coupling form the numerical core; VOF–PLIC interface geometry and continuum-surface-force capillarity resolve the connected interfaces; and interphase exchange, breakup, and phase-change closures couple the fields. The Ransom faucet problem, SPHERIC Test 2, and a three-dimensional rising-bubble benchmark verify the transport, free-surface, and capillary treatments, while FARO L-24 validates the complete model under prototypical corium–water conditions. On the finest SPHERIC grid, the median NRMSE is 0.159 over the four water-level records and 0.139 over the eight pressure records, and the first P1 impact amplitude is reproduced within 10.2 %. The rising-bubble calculation preserves the gas volume and reproduces the published centroid, velocity, sphericity, and directional-size endpoints to within 5 %. In the 15-point FARO pressure comparison, the steam-only calculation has an RMSE of about 0.18 MPa and reproduces the approximately 2.2 MPa peak near 1.6 s; adding the reported L-24 hydrogen source raises the late-time pressure by about 0.48 MPa and quantifies the coupled sensitivity of the steam, noncondensable-gas, and headspace closures. The calculation also yields the material inventories, energy partition, void, azimuthal asymmetry, and a debris-size distribution consistent with the post-test recovery. These results establish the premixing capability of SEINA-3D and provide three-dimensional mixture states for steam-explosion load analysis.

English abstract

Precise characterization of the fast-neutron energy spectrum constitutes a fundamental requirement for evaluating radiation damage in reactor pressure vessels (RPV). Conventional MLEM iterative adjustment algorithms applied to high-precision spectrum adjustment often result in potential spectral artifacts due to the inability to rigorously propagate off-diagonal covariance terms. A novel Bayesian Regularized Maximum Likelihood Expectation Maximization (BR-MLEM) framework is proposed to address these challenges by integrating hierarchical Bayesian priors with adaptive regularization. This methodology systematically incorporates covariance matrices associated with the prior spectrum, response functions, and measurements, employing singular value decomposition (SVD)-based Monte Carlo sampling to mitigate noise amplification. Application to the H. B. Robinson Unit 2 benchmark demonstrates the algorithm’s capability to produce physically consistent spectra while effectively suppressing non-physical distortions observed in standard MLEM iterations. The adjusted calculated specific activity exhibits excellent agreement with experimental dosimetry data, achieving consistency comparable to generalized least-squares methods but with superior stability in high-energy regions. Posterior uncertainty analysis reveals a significant reduction in relative standard deviations across primary fast-neutron energy groups. These findings confirm that the BR-MLEM framework enhances the fidelity of fluence estimation and provides a robust tool for RPV lifetime management.

English abstract

The three-dimensional power distribution (3DPD) of a reactor core is an important indicator of reactor operating conditions, and its accurate reconstruction is essential for core monitoring and safe operation. Compared with conventional large reactors, small reactors with hexagonal fuel assemblies exhibit stronger neutron leakage and more complex spatial power characteristics, increasing the difficulty of power reconstruction. To address this issue, this study investigates 3DPD in a hexagonal-assembly small pressurized water reactor using the thin-plate spline (TPS) method combined with in-core detector response functions. The results show that the in-core detector response exhibits strong spatial localization characteristics. Radially, the detector response is mainly concentrated within the third ring of neighboring assemblies (∼15 cm). Neglecting detector response leads to significant reconstruction errors. By incorporating detector response functions, the TPS method effectively suppresses reconstruction deviations under different burnup levels, keeping all post-reconstruction relative errors within the commonly adopted 8% acceptance criterion. The proposed method demonstrates good applicability to hexagonal fuel assembly 3DPD reconstruction in small reactors.

Repeated factorization of the complex shifted systems can therefore dominate the computational cost of the Chebyshev rational approximation method (CRAM). This study develops a direct block solver based on strongly connected component (SCC) decomposition and implements it in MACT.
