English abstract

The main goal is to show the feasibility of reducing the radiological hazard associated with fission products and long-lived radionuclides in spent fuel rods through non-invasive processing using fission and radiative neutron capture reactions within a selective neutron-energy system. A setup involving 50 spent fuel rods as targets was analyzed, considering neutron fluence, radionuclide removal rates, effective removal constants, effective half-lives, and criticality safety. Radionuclide removal was evaluated by exposing the fuel to neutron spectra covering well-resolved resonances or cold-neutron 1/v region at 5±1 meV. Reaction rates from analytical calculations were confirmed using MCNP, with discrepancies below 5%. Effective removal constants and corresponding half-lives were evaluated under operational conditions with 1012 n/s. The calculated effective half-lives for U-235, Pu-239, Pu-240, Pu-241, Pu-242, Cm-244, Am-241, Np-237, I-129, Tc-99, and Sm-151 were less than 1y. The radiotoxicity decreases by three-orders of magnitude over 2×108s timescale. These findings underscore the potential of neutron-spectrum tailoring as an effective strategy for managing long-lived radionuclides in spent nuclear fuel within practical timescales.

This study proposes a novel Reliability-Based Emergency Planning Zone Assessment (RB-EPZA) framework integrating Gaussian plume dispersion, uncertainty quantification, and structural reliability concepts. Twelve random variables were modeled using Normal, Lognormal, and Weibull distributions, and the probability of non-compliance was estimated through Monte Carlo Simulation with 107 realizations. The proposed RB-EPZA framework provides a transparent and extensible methodology for uncertainty-informed and risk-informed EPZ assessment.

English abstract

Accurate identification of thermal parameters in multilayer thermal protection structures (MTPS) is challenging because the multilayer configuration and temperature dependence result in a high-dimensional parameter space involving 27 uncertain parameters, including thermal properties and thermal contact resistance (TCR). This study develops an efficient neural network surrogate-assisted Bayesian framework for thermal parameter identification in a four-layer MTPS. To alleviate parameter coupling and ill-posedness, the inverse problem is divided into four independent sub-processes rather than identifying all unknown parameters simultaneously. In each sub-process, an artificial neural network surrogate is first employed to replace repeated and costly numerical simulations, and sensitivity analysis is then performed to determine critical parameters and informative time intervals, thereby reducing the number of parameters simultaneously considered and enabling their sequential identification using a Bayesian network.
