English abstract

Molten salt reactors (MSRs) present specific challenges for safety analysis, arising from the liquid and circulating nature of the fuel, the strong coupling between neutronics and thermohydraulics, and the potential presence of gases. During rapid reactivity insertions, compressibility effects — including pressure and shock wave propagation — can play a significant role that is not captured by conventional incompressible or quasi-incompressible approaches. This work presents Cognacc, a reduced-order coupled neutronics–thermohydraulics code developed for fast transient simulations of MSRs. The thermohydraulic module solves the compressible homogeneous relaxation model (HRM) equations using an HLLC Riemann solver, closed by the Noble–Abel Stiffened Gas equation of state, enabling accurate representation of pressure waves, phase change, and non-condensable gases. A dedicated equation of state for an actinide chloride molten salt is derived from molecular dynamics calculations. The neutronic module is based on improved point kinetics equations accounting for the convective transport of delayed neutron precursors throughout the fuel circuit. The code is verified and preliminary validated through a progressive procedure: classical shock-tube test cases confirm the correct numerical implementation; the CANON depressurization experiment validates the phase-change and variable flow area models; and code-to-code comparisons against LiCore under slow transient conditions verify the multiphysics coupling. The results demonstrate the ability of Cognacc to accurately reproduce coupled neutronics–thermohydraulics transients while maintaining low computational cost, supporting its use as an exploratory tool for MSR design and safety studies.

English abstract

Flow-induced vibration of nuclear fuel rods is a critical issue affecting the structural integrity, operational safety, and long-term reliability of advanced nuclear energy systems. However, high-fidelity fluid–structure interaction simulations required for accurate flow-induced vibration prediction are often computationally expensive, limiting their use in rapid assessment and design optimization. To address this challenge, this study proposes a reduced-order model based on component mode synthesis and proper orthogonal decomposition for reconstructing full-field flow-induced vibration responses of fuel rods from sparse measurements. The fuel rod is first partitioned into subcomponents according to the locations of measurement and target points, and the structural degrees of freedom are reduced using the Craig-Bampton component mode synthesis method. Static loading cases are then designed to construct snapshot matrices, from which dominant modes are extracted. Based on sparse measured displacement responses, the corresponding mode coefficients are identified through Tikhonov regularization, enabling efficient prediction of full-field dynamic responses. The proposed method is applied to circular and helical cruciform fuel rods immersed in lead–bismuth eutectic coolant. Results show that the method accurately predicts both natural frequencies and flow-induced vibration responses, achieving model reduction ratios of 98.21 percent and 97.81 percent, respectively. Even under 20 percent measurement noise, the coefficients of determination remain as high as 0.957 and 0.934, demonstrating strong robustness and prediction accuracy. The proposed method provides an efficient and reliable tool for rapid flow-induced vibration response prediction and can support the vibration-resistant design and safety assessment of nuclear fuel rods in advanced energy systems.

English abstract

High-temperature thermo-mechanical experiments were conducted to investigate the behavior of self-developed new type zirconium cladding under loss-of-coolant accident(LOCA)conditions,including high-temperature oxidation,high-temperature creep,high-temperature burst,and embrittlement experiment.Results show that the high-temperature oxidation rate of self-developed new type zirconium cladding is comparable to that of ZIRLO,M5,and Zr-4 cladding when the oxidation temperature exceeds 1000°C,which can be estimated by Cathcart-Pawel model.Notably,the self-developed new type zirconium cladding exhibits significantly lower high-temperature creep-burst strain than M5 and Zr-4 cladding,which can decrease fuel assembly blockage during LOCA,thus contributing to a more favorable outcome in LOCA analysis.Additionally,the embrittlement criteria of the self-developed new type zirconium cladding meet the requirements of a peak cladding temperature of≤1204℃and the maximum cladding oxidation rate of≤17%.
